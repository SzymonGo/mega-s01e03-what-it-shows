parser: the shared parser reads the month first

Reports: 379, counted the desk’s way: by tenant, so the one-day moves below are in here too. Every tenant in #4471 writes the day first, and the parser reads every date month first, so 7 April goes out as 4 July. These come in by app, by email, and through booking-v2’s form, whose hint already says DD/MM/YYYY. Fixed with fix(dates): parse dd/mm for eu locales; #1 is closed.

hint: booking-v3 tells people to type the month first

Reports: 33, all through the form of the three tenants on booking-v3. Its date field prints MM/DD/YYYY; people follow it, and the form reads the date the tenant’s way, day first. The result looks the same as parser, a month flipped, but the parser fix does not touch it. Won’t fix: booking-v3 is retired, and its hint goes with it; #2 is closed.

one-day-move: bookings slip by a day

Reports: 5, and nothing flipped in them. The booking moved by one day: Tuesday in Lisbon and Monday in Toronto for the same meeting, an invite that arrived before the booking, a tenant who sees it only in June. That points at time zones, not date order. The desk read these as a one-day move (desk-notes.json) and files them under parser by tenant, so they count in both places; the parser fix will not close them. One reporter says they have stopped reporting these, so the count is a floor. Still open in #3.

Why one ticket hid three

The desk merges on subject line (decision 0001). All three problems arrive as “wrong day on the invite”, so subject matching put them together. The cause depends on the tenant and the way in (causes.ts), and the subject says neither. Intake’s tag guesses from the reporter’s browser and disagrees with the tenant rule too; the table above shows both.
